Wales have beaten New Zealand at last. A 35-28 win at Cardiff's Principality Stadium on Saturday ended a run of 34 straight defeats against the All Blacks, stretching back to 1953.

It was a cracking result, and a hard-earned one. Wales led by 14 points on three separate occasions and scored five tries against New Zealand for the first time in their history. In the end, though, it was stubborn defending that kept the visitors at bay. The win came a week after Wales had ground out a victory over Japan.

A crowd of 55,152 watched as players wept and the scale of the moment sank in. It is arguably the most famous win in Wales's 145-year Test history. Prince William, patron of the Welsh Rugby Union, called it a "fantastic result by the Wales rugby team".

Supporters had stories of their own. John Jenkins, now 81, was eight when Wales last won this fixture and had no television to watch it on. He lives in Shropshire but travels to every match, and said this was the most memorable of all the big Welsh occasions he has attended. He said his daughter Hannah, 50, who travels from Chichester, sobbed on his shoulder for ten minutes after the final whistle.

Elsewhere, a wedding in Carmarthen put its speeches back by 15 minutes so that guests could see out the match. Photographer Rhian Jones said the entire wedding breakfast was on their phones.

Kyle Aston's 102-year-old grandfather, a devoted rugby fan, was 29 when Wales last beat the All Blacks. He fell asleep just before the final whistle after all that waiting, though Aston said that, with matches available to watch back, he will be able to relive the win.

At The Cottage pub on St Mary's Street in Cardiff, Warren Atkins said the place was already packed before kick-off, but the celebrations afterwards went up another level. He was getting in more beer for the following week's game.

First Minister Rhun ap Iorwerth said the long wait had become a psychological block for the nation. He said a new generation could now move forward knowing that Wales can beat New Zealand.

## Why it matters

Rugby sits close to the heart of Welsh national life, and the fixture against New Zealand carries particular weight for a small country with a proud sporting tradition. A drought lasting generations is hard on supporters, so the release of it was shared by fans of every age, in the stadium, in pubs and rugby clubs, and even around a wedding table. Moments like this bring families and communities together, and give a nation something to remember for years to come.
