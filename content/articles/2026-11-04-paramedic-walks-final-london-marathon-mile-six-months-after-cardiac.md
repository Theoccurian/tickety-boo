A paramedic whose heart stopped during the London Marathon has now walked the last mile he never got to finish. Gareth Hopkins, 43, from Hoddesdon in Hertfordshire, made his way along The Mall on Sunday, six months after he collapsed, with family, friends and well-wishers beside him.

Gareth was a first-time marathon runner when he went into cardiac arrest at mile 25 on 26 April. The cause was severe rhabdomyolysis, brought on by overheating. He has no memory of the day.

He was treated in intensive care at St Thomas' Hospital and remained under the hospital's care for six months, getting home only in September. Afterwards he had to relearn the everyday basics, from walking to speaking to eating. Taking on the final mile a month after leaving hospital was no mean feat, and he admitted to feeling nervous beforehand.

He reckons about 200 people turned out to see him through the last stretch, a number he found faintly absurd. He brushed aside the talk of heroism, saying he was just glad it had gone far better than he had expected.

Gareth had been running with his brother Chris, 41, from Whitwell, to raise money for Age UK. Their grandmother, Tricia Petts, died in January 2025, and the brothers chose the charity as a tribute to her. Chris had already finished his race when the family, who had been following Gareth's progress, saw that he had stopped and went to look for him. They found him with a St John Ambulance team, who Chris says helped bring his brother back from cardiac arrest.

Chris now plans a third London Marathon in 2027, raising money for St John Ambulance. Gareth is not yet well enough to work, and a fundraising page for his family has collected almost £36,000, a welcome boost. His own Age UK total stands at £2,900 against a £3,000 target.

Gareth says his marathon days are over, and that he has been told not to run. His wife, Jodie, was beside him throughout, and he thanked everyone who helped save him. "I know exactly how lucky I got," he said.

Age UK's fundraising director, Hannorah Lee, said it was special to watch him complete the mile and praised his generosity and determination.

## Why it matters

Surviving a cardiac arrest is far from certain, and the outcome often depends on how quickly trained people can step in. Gareth's story shows the value of first-aid teams at mass-participation events, and how long and demanding recovery can be even after a life has been saved. It also shows a community rallying round one person and the charities he set out to support.
