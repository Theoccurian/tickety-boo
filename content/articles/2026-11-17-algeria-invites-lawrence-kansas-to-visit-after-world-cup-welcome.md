A party of Kansans stepped off their plane in Algeria at 4:30 one morning in early November and found dozens of officials and hospitality representatives waiting with flowers. It was Algeria's way of repaying the welcome that Lawrence, Kansas, gave its football team during the 2026 World Cup.

Algeria had chosen Lawrence as its base for the tournament. Hundreds of people from the Algerian diaspora across North America then converged on the small city. A young woman studying at the nearby University of Kansas organised much of it, and with her friends she set up an Instagram account and Facebook page called L'Algerie fi Kansas City to rally support for the team's arrival at the airport.

One video captured the mood better than most. A Lawrence resident, speaking to Algerian journalists travelling with the squad, thanked the country for choosing his city. He admitted that he and his neighbours knew little about Algeria, but said they wanted the visitors to feel at home. The clip was watched tens of millions of times.

Goodwill flowed both ways. Aaron Downey, a local who follows international club football, said: "I think the Algerians adopted us."

The President of Algeria has now returned the compliment with an invitation. The travelling group included Lawrence's mayor, Brad Finkeldei, members of the fan group, officers from the local police who had kept the team safe during training, and some residents of neighbouring Kansas City.

The guests carried passes reading Delegation Member and were driven around to see the sights. They visited Tipaza, a UNESCO World Heritage Site with Roman and Carthaginian ruins, and the Royal Mausoleum of Mauretania. One of the Kansans said in a video shared by Algiers Airport that he had never imagined so many people would be standing by the plane to greet them at such an hour.

Football, naturally, was on the programme. The Kansans cheered on Les Fennecs as they edged past Togo by a single goal in qualifying for the African Cup of Nations, which made for a splendid day out.

## Why it matters

Major sporting tournaments are often framed around rivalry, yet this story shows how they can also link communities that would never otherwise meet. A modest American city and a North African nation have now exchanged hospitality in both directions, a reminder that a simple, generous welcome can grow into lasting friendship.
