A woman from New Hampshire has passed the one-year mark with a gene-edited pig kidney working inside her body. As far as is known, no other patient has kept such an organ for longer. Molly Falco, 58, reached the anniversary on Sunday.

Surgeons at Mass General Brigham in Boston carried out her transplant. The hospital's team says the new kidney clears waste from her bloodstream, produces urine and helps hold the balance of fluids and minerals in her body.

Dr Leonardo Riella, who heads kidney transplant research at the hospital and oversees her care, says her progress shows that these organs can do the job. He is careful not to get ahead of himself, however, and says it is too early to celebrate. His hope is that the kidney will carry on for at least another year.

Falco was born with a condition that harmed her kidneys, and she spent 11 months on dialysis before the operation. Some complications arose soon after surgery, but she has not needed to stay in hospital since February.

Her days are now close to normal. She tires quickly, can become breathless and wears a mask in public to guard against infection, while her medicines leave her with a slight tremor. Even so, she is cooking meals for her family, meeting friends and driving again, none of which was possible on dialysis. Doctors have cleared her to visit relatives in Colorado for Christmas.

"I'm amazed every day that it's still working," she said. She has named the kidney Lily, after the flowers her Colorado relatives sent when she came out of surgery.

More evidence should follow soon. In January, a formal trial of pig kidneys made by the biotech company eGenesis is due to begin, with 33 patients across 10 medical centres in the United States. Meanwhile, United Therapeutics started its own kidney trial at NYU Langone Health last year. It now has a second kidney study under way and has won approval for a small trial of pig hearts in people with heart failure.

## Why it matters

Kidney failure affects a great many people, and donor organs are scarce. About 90,000 people in the United States are waiting for a kidney, yet fewer than 26,000 transplants were done last year, and on average 12 people die each day before an organ turns up. Pig organs are still experimental and one patient's experience cannot settle the question, but a year of steady function is a welcome boost for those on the list. A Canadian bioethics researcher called it a milestone worth celebrating, while pointing out that recruiting more organ donors and encouraging living donation are probably cheaper and easier routes for now.
