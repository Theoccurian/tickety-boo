Rather good news from Cornwall: a seal called Jarvis has been judged well enough by vets to rejoin his companions at the Cornish Seal Sanctuary in Gweek, following successful cataract surgery on his right eye.

Staff say the move back into his usual pool went smoothly. He was reintroduced over the weekend and is now settling in with fellow residents Bo and Buddy.

Jarvis came to the sanctuary in 2016, when he was rescued with cataracts affecting both eyes. In October, a specialist eye vet operated on his right eye.

The sanctuary had asked the public to help cover the cost of operating on both eyes, so that he might have the chance to see again. The appeal raised more than £15,000, a welcome boost for the plan.

Clare Addyman, an animal care specialist at the sanctuary, said Jarvis was "doing really great". He still needs eye drops, and his eyes are a little sore, she explained, but things were going well so far.

The cloudiness in the operated eye has mostly cleared. According to Addyman, there are fairly obvious signs that he can see much better with it than before.

His left eye has not yet been operated on. Sanctuary bosses had said earlier that they hoped to carry out that surgery in spring 2027, which would allow his sight to be fully restored.

## Why it matters

Cataracts cloud the lens of the eye and, if left untreated, can seriously reduce vision, in animals as in people. Jarvis's recovery so far suggests the operation on his right eye has made a real difference to what he can see. For the sanctuary's carers and the supporters who donated, it is an encouraging step towards the longer-term aim of restoring sight in both eyes.
