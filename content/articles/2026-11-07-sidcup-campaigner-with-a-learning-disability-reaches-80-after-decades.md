Geoffrey Leer has reached his 80th year, and he is making the most of it. A former campaigner for people with learning disabilities, he lives in a care home in Sidcup, south-east London, and has lately appeared on Channel 4 News.

For his interview with the charity Mencap, which also turned 80 this year, he looked dapper in a navy suit jacket, a handkerchief tucked into the pocket and a dark green knitted jumper. He came in with his walker, a twinkle in his eye and a broad smile, and a nurse helped him into his chair.

Mencap's research indicates that only a tenth of people with a learning disability live to see 80. Leer is well aware of how unusual that is. He says you seldom hear about people with learning disabilities reaching that age, and he has a firm view on the milestone: "It's the golden age, innit?"

Life has brought changes. Leer has dementia and has lost much of his sight, yet he stays positive and speaks warmly about all he has seen and done.

His working life started early. He left school at 17 to help support his family and held a variety of jobs over many years. Later he moved into leadership, sharing the chair of the Learning Disability Partnership Board and serving as a trustee of Bexley Mencap.

Across more than three decades, he spoke up for people with a learning disability. He worked with local schools, talked to stakeholders at the local council and promoted campaigns to raise awareness of life with a learning disability.

Reaching 80 is no mean feat for anyone, and Leer has done it while staying cheerful and ready for the camera.

## Why it matters

Mencap's figure shows that reaching 80 is uncommon for people with a learning disability, so Leer's story is a chance to hear from someone who has. His years of work in schools and with the council also show how people with lived experience can help others understand what a learning disability means. Leer's own remark, that such stories are rarely told, is a good reason to share his.
