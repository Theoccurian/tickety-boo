A Texas community has thanked Gloria Kennard, known to many as "Miss Gloria", for 37 years of Thanksgiving dinners by buying her a new car. Friends and supporters gathered at her home on Friday 30 October to unveil a 2027 Kia Seltos SUV, paid for with more than $27,000 in donations.

Kennard started cooking her holiday meal in 1988, while she was working at Texas A&M University. She said she felt prompted to do something to help someone, and the Thanksgiving dinner was the idea she came up with. It has grown steadily ever since and now feeds more than 2,000 people.

The meal is not her only commitment. She also prepares a New Year's Day dinner, and for the past 16 years she has taken weekly volunteer shifts at The Bridge Ministries food pantry.

Then, on 29 July, another vehicle collided with her truck as she drove to Bible study and choir rehearsal. The truck was totalled, and her insurance payout was not enough to replace it. She was left relying on friends for lifts, which is a real loss of independence for someone whose days revolve around serving others.

The Bridge Ministries decided to act and set up a GoFundMe page with a target of $15,000, hoping to find a vehicle that was safe and reliable. Emma Bridges, the charity's Development and Outreach Coordinator, said Kennard has always given freely of her time, energy and treasures, so the community felt it only right to do the same for her.

The response went well beyond the target. By October, 174 donors had given more than $17,700, and a local business, Kent Moore Cabinets, added $10,000. Pushing the total past $27,000 was no mean feat.

Bridges said every dollar represented a life Kennard had touched, and pointed to the comments on the fundraising page, some from people who had known her for more than 40 years.

Standing beside the new SUV in her driveway, a visibly delighted Kennard thanked the community and wished them God's blessing. The surprise came just weeks before her next Thanksgiving feast.

## Why it matters

For anyone who volunteers in person, reliable transport is often what makes the work possible. In this case, a community saw that a car accident had put both a woman's independence and her service to others at risk, and it came together to put things right. It shows how years of steady generosity can inspire the same in return.
