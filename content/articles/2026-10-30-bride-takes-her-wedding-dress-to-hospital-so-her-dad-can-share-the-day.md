A bride from Greater Manchester spent part of her wedding day on a hospital ward, so that her father, who was too unwell to be at the ceremony, could still see her in her dress and share a dance.

Sinèad Jackson, 36, from Dukinfield, was due to marry Stephen, her partner of 19 years, at the Village Hotel in Hyde on 10 October. Two days beforehand, her father, Leslie Healy, was admitted to Tameside General Hospital with heart problems. She had always pictured him walking her down the aisle.

When she visited him on the ward the day before the wedding, a nurse told her his condition was serious. Because the risk was so high, he could not be allowed out, not even for an hour. Leslie, a stubborn sort by his daughter's account, would not hear of a postponement. He told her to get on with the day and promised to look at the photographs and videos afterwards.

Sinèad was not ready to leave it there. After a quick word with the nursing team, she arranged to drop in on the wedding day itself, taking her mum and the wedding photographer, Jen. She reached the hospital at 15:00, an hour before the ceremony, and had not even checked a mirror on her way out of the hotel.

Leslie was dressed in his wedding suit and waiting for her. She showed him her dress, and the two of them danced to *This Dance* by Scott Thomas Laughridge. Footage captures them swaying slowly together.

Staff on the ward entered into the spirit of things, and several asked to take her picture. Some patients were photographed too. "I'd do it all over again if I could," Sinèad said.

She arrived at the ceremony a little late, but everything went smoothly after that. Maxie, who she said was standing in for his grandad, gave her away, and all of her brothers were present. Seeing her dad first, she said, let her relax.

Rather good news followed: Leslie has since been discharged and is recovering at home. He is delighted with the pictures. He said the family were grateful to the hospital staff for arranging the visit, which let everyone celebrate together.

## Why it matters

Hospital teams must put patient safety first, and here the medical advice was that Leslie should stay on the ward. The story shows how a bit of goodwill and flexibility from staff can still bring families together at life's big moments when plans change. Small gestures like this can mean a great deal to patients and their relatives.
