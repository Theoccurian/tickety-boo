David Carbert, who is 87, has completed his 400th Parkrun at Humber Bridge Country Park, and he has no plans to hang up his trainers just yet.

He first turned up ten years ago, aged 77. Staying indoors with little to occupy him did not appeal, so he decided to give the weekly event a go. He was nervous that first morning and worried about embarrassing himself, but he says the other runners made him feel at home. In his words, it is "one big happy family".

These days, Carbert gets up early on Saturdays and does some stretches before he leaves the house. He turns out whatever the weather, with only deep snow or ice enough to keep him away.

His personal best of 28 minutes and 13 seconds came when he was 80. He now typically finishes the 5km (3.1-mile) route in about 38 minutes, which is no mean feat at his age. He describes himself as fairly fit and well, and he believes all that running may have added years to his life.

Helen Penn, the event's director, said the whole community adores him and called him an inspiration. She also praised his dedication and suggested he is only just getting started.

Tony Nicholson, who has run alongside him for years, regards Carbert as a role model. He did admit, with a laugh, that it irritates him that Carbert wins their sprint to the line every week.

Once he had crossed the finish line for the 400th time, Carbert was a little out of breath and said he felt shattered, though he expected to recover soon enough.

His next goal is 500 Parkruns. By the time he gets there, he will be nearly 90, which is a cracking ambition.

## Why it matters

Staying active in later life is widely linked with better physical health, and regular company can matter just as much as the exercise itself. Carbert's story shows how a friendly, community-run event can make it easier for people of any age to get started and keep going. One decision to leave the house has grown into ten years of routine and friendship.
