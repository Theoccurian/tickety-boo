A Navy veteran in Minneapolis who has been without a car of his own for more than two years has been handed the keys to a refurbished 2020 Hyundai Elantra, restored by students at a local college.

Robert Hanson collected the car at the Dunwoody College of Engineering. Trainees from the college's Automotive Collision and Refinishing school had spent several months on the bodywork, and the handover showed them exactly who their efforts had been for.

Mr Hanson served as a Navy airman from 1993 to 1997 and has had serious health problems since leaving the military. Severe asthma and fibromyalgia make public transport extremely difficult for him. A Hennepin County welfare service put him forward for the Recycled Rides for Schools programme, which is run by the nonprofit National Auto Body Council.

The Elantra was donated by Allstate, one of the council's partners. The company had recovered it after a crash that took away a sizeable chunk of the front end, according to images of the damage, so putting it right was no mean feat.

Mr Hanson said he was overwhelmed and grateful to everyone involved. Until now he has been walking everywhere to get his food, but with a car of his own he can shop at more affordable places and buy in bulk. He expects to get out and enjoy life again.

Collision student Abbie Vera said she was glad to see how pleased he was with what was, to all intents and purposes, a brand-new car, and happy that the students had played a part. It is a cracking result for everyone concerned.

## Why it matters

Reliable transport is easy to take for granted, but for someone with a long-term health condition it can decide whether shopping, appointments and seeing other people are manageable. Schemes like this meet a practical need for a veteran, while showing trainee technicians that their skills can make a real difference to someone's everyday life.
