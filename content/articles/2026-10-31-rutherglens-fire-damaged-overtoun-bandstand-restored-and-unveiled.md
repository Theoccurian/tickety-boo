The Overtoun bandstand in Rutherglen has been formally unveiled after a painstaking restoration, two years on from a fire that very nearly destroyed it. The category B-listed structure is 112 years old and was badly damaged by a blaze in June 2024.

The fire consumed every bit of the timber. Fortunately, enough of the iron frame was left standing to allow an accurate rebuild.

South Lanarkshire Council brought in Covanburn Contracts, a firm specialising in heritage restoration. Its craftsmen reconstructed the original design from the remaining ironwork, old photographs and rare foundry catalogues, a task they likened to solving a jigsaw of history.

The ornate ironwork was the trickiest part, and no mean feat. It was first cast by the Saracen Foundry in Possilpark, Glasgow, which was once seen as Scotland's foremost maker of decorative ironwork. Pieces from the foundry survive as far afield as Brazil and Australia, and closer to home in Glasgow's Kibble Palace.

Since the foundry shut in the 1960s, a good deal of its paperwork has vanished. The team therefore had to set old photographs beside the catalogues that remained, and work out the missing details from there.

Covanburn's managing director, Alex Stewart, said the job demanded a great deal of planning and research before work in Overtoun Park could begin in June. He said seeing the restoration take shape made that effort worthwhile.

Eugénie Aroutcheff, co-founder of the local charity Grow73, said residents feel a real emotional bond with the bandstand, and many associate it with childhood days or a first kiss. She is chuffed that it is ready for the town's 900th anniversary, and says many people feel Rutherglen is a rather overlooked corner of Glasgow. King David I granted the town royal burgh status in 1126.

The bandstand has been returned to its original look, with a single new feature. A mosaic set into the concrete base was designed alongside pupils from nearby Stonelaw High School. It displays the town motto, Ex fumo fama, which means fame from smoke.

Police Scotland regarded the fire as deliberate when it happened. Officers carried out extensive enquiries but the person responsible has not been traced, and the force says it will assess any fresh information.

## Why it matters

Historic structures are hard to replace once lost, and this one has been saved through careful detective work as well as skilled craftsmanship. Rebuilding it faithfully gives Rutherglen back a well-loved public landmark in its anniversary year. The mosaic created with local young people also links the town's past with its next generation.
