Fully electric cars accounted for a record 31% of new passenger car registrations in Europe in September, with 422,000 finding owners. That is 63% more than a year earlier, and the quickest annual growth the market has seen in over three years.

Plug-in hybrids had a good month too, rising 23% to a record 167,000. Put the two together and plug-in vehicles reached a best-ever 590,000 registrations, or 43% of the market, up 49% on the year.

Across the year so far, pure electrics hold a 25% share, already ahead of the 20% recorded for the whole of 2025. Counting plug-in hybrids as well, the figure is 36%, against 29% last year.

Petrol and diesel carried on losing ground. Petrol took 18% of September sales, down 21%, while diesel managed 4%, down 28%. Conventional hybrids without a plug still grew by 5%, but the overall market grew by 10% to 1.4 million cars, so their share slipped from 36% to 34%. That may hint they have passed their peak.

It was rather good news for Tesla. The Model Y was the best-selling car of any kind in Europe, with 41,987 registrations, up 52% and better than any month in the previous four and a half years. The Model 3 came second overall with 26,770, a leap of 120%. Only once before, in December 2022, has Tesla taken the top two places across the entire market. Buyers of the Model 3 currently face a wait of three to four months.

Others were on the up as well. BYD's Seal U reached a record 12,779, helped by generous discounts, and the Skoda Elroq also set a record with 12,592. The BYD Atto 2 followed on 11,111, largely thanks to a new plug-in hybrid version.

Electrification is spreading beyond the usual names. Some 59% of Mercedes GLC sales were electric or plug-in hybrid versions, and the Renault Twingo took second place among the smallest cars. The weak spot is the B-segment, where none of the top three models has a plug-in version yet.

## Why it matters

Road transport is a major source of carbon emissions, so each petrol or diesel purchase replaced by an electric one helps cut pollution over the life of the car. With the Model 3 starting around €35,000 and the Model Y from €40,000, buyers appear to be choosing electric on value as well as principle. Europe still has some way to go, particularly among smaller cars, but the direction of travel is clear.
