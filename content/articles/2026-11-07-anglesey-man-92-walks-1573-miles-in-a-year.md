John Morgan Jôb, a sprightly 92-year-old from Llanfairpwll on Anglesey, has walked 1,573 miles in a single year. His smartwatch reckons that is the same distance as 60 marathons.

The message from the watch, congratulating him on the total, came as quite a shock. Assuming there must be some mistake, he sent a message to Google, and was amazed to find the figure was true.

His aim is 30 miles a week, which comes to about 120 miles a month. He did more at one time, he says, but at 92 he marvels, and is thankful, that he can still manage it at all.

Consistency is his secret. He walks six days a week, setting off at 7.30am with a rucksack holding waterproofs and a bottle of water. If the weather is not too bad, he covers about five miles towards neighbouring villages such as Gaerwen and Brynsiencyn, or along the Menai Strait to Menai Bridge, and is home by about 9.30am. Sunday is a rest day for his body, and he makes sure he is at chapel by 10am.

Mr Jôb was a minister for decades and has kept active all his life. He played football as a boy in Pont-iets, Carmarthenshire, and later took up running, cycling and golf.

His advice goes back to the evening before he moved to Anglesey, after retiring as a minister in Swansea. His GP, an Irishman he remembers as most interesting, called round to say farewell and urged him not to sit about too much or watch too much television. Aim first to be fit, the doctor told him, and then super fit.

Mr Jôb says walking does his spirits as much good as his body. It makes him feel happier, he says, and he likens it to a drug. It is the first thing he thinks about each morning.

There is a social side too. Chatting to people along his routes has led to friendships with people he would never otherwise have met, which, he says, adds to the value of the day.

## Why it matters

Regular walking is widely recognised as a low-impact way to look after the heart, joints and mood, and it is open to many people later in life. Mr Jôb's story shows how a modest habit, kept up steadily, can add up to a long distance over a year. It also shows that a friendly word with neighbours along the way can be good for wellbeing.
