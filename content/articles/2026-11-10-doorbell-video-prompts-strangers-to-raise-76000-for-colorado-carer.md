A short doorbell-camera clip has led more than 2,600 strangers to donate over $76,000 to a woman who spends her days looking after other people.

Suzanna Metcalf arrived last year at the Castle Rock, Colorado, home of Pastor Neal Seiwert with the wrong pizza order. She apologised, explained that she would have to go back for the correct one, and blamed her age. The strain on her face told a different story.

Metcalf works delivery shifts and is also the full-time carer for her 82-year-old mother. Her days take in daily care, medical needs and the running costs of the household.

Seiwert, his wife Hailee and their three young daughters did not grumble about the mix-up. Instead, they came out onto the doorstep, offered her a prayer and laid comforting hands on her shoulders. The camera caught Metcalf visibly moved at the end of a long day's work.

Seiwert shared the footage online, and it reached millions of viewers. Hundreds of them left comments asking much the same thing: how could they help?

The family got in touch with Metcalf and, with her agreement, set up a GoFundMe page. It describes a woman who has long put others before herself. The aim was a modest $10,000, but over the following year the total sailed past $76,000, a cracking result.

Seiwert said he wanted Metcalf to know that his family was behind her, and that plenty of other people value her too. He added that many viewers had been moved to tears by the clip.

The money is meant to ease the pressure on Metcalf's finances while she carries on caring for her mother.

## Why it matters

Family carers often shoulder heavy practical and financial burdens with little recognition, and many fit paid work around round-the-clock care. A viral clip cannot lift those demands, but this story shows how one small, unprompted act of kindness can persuade thousands of strangers to turn sympathy into practical help, and give one stretched carer a welcome boost.
