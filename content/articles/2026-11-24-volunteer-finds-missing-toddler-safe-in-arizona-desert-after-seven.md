A volunteer searcher in Arizona has been honoured by the local sheriff after finding a missing two-year-old girl safe and well in the desert, just after midnight.

Vella Campillo had been playing with her siblings at sundown on 17 November when she wandered off into the desert around the city of Eloy. She was wearing only a nappy. Her parents, Joseph Campillo and Deanna Fritz, contacted the police shortly after 5pm.

The Pinal County Sheriff's Office and local volunteers set out as darkness fell and the temperature dropped. Among them was Jay Stewart, a father and grandfather. Over the following seven hours, he and his fellow volunteers, alongside dozens of officers from several law enforcement agencies, searched with flashlights in temperatures of about 50°F.

Drones and a helicopter joined in, but the thermal imaging cameras were of little use, because such a small child gave off so little heat to detect.

Shortly after midnight, Mr Stewart was checking a row of bushes beside a ditch when he came upon Vella curled up inside. He called the sheriff's office to report the news. Getting her off to hospital took a good deal of coaxing, since she had attached herself firmly to her rescuer and had no wish to let go.

She was soon back with her family, with no injuries and no hypothermia, which is a cracking result after a night in the open.

Sheriff Ross Teeple later praised Mr Stewart in a video posted online, naming him a hero of the county. He presented him with a certificate recognising him as an outstanding citizen and made him an honorary member of the sheriff's rescue posse. Mr Stewart called the whole experience life-changing.

Vella's parents said they felt hugely fortunate to have had his help on what they described as the worst day of their lives. Mr Stewart said he had simply shared the feeling of everyone else out searching: that any child in that position could easily have been their own.

## Why it matters

Searches for lost young children are a race against time, and a cold desert night makes them more urgent still. This story shows how much trained professionals can gain from willing volunteers, especially when technology reaches its limits, as the heat-sensing cameras did here. It is also a reminder that ordinary people giving up a night for someone else's child can change the outcome for a whole family.
