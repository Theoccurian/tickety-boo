A glitter-strewn dance contest in Stoke-on-Trent has brought in more than £200,000 for the Dougie Mac hospice charity, the largest sum any single event has raised in the organisation's history.

Strictly Knot Dancing, an annual fixture, took place on Friday at the city's King's Hall. The total has already overtaken the £145,000 achieved last year, and the final figure may yet climb as the remaining donations are counted.

Twelve local amateurs were each partnered with a professional dancer. They performed for around 600 spectators and a panel of expert judges, all hoping to lift the glitterball trophy, in a format that will be familiar to fans of BBC's Strictly Come Dancing.

Much of the money was in the bank before the music even started, with over £120,000 raised ahead of the first routine. On the night, guests could also bid in a silent auction, try their luck on a tombola and join in other games, all to swell the pot for the charity, which is based in Blurton.

Shanie Chaudry-Goodall was crowned overall winner after impressing the judges. Fellow contestant Jenny Johnstone described her dance as phenomenal and said the victory was richly deserved.

Johnstone said the group had been elated at the finish, and proud to be among the 12 who had raised such a sum. In her view, a polished show is what encourages an audience to give a little more. She admitted to a bout of nerves just before her own dance, but said she loved every second.

Matt Holdcroft, who finished ahead of Johnstone, said the room seemed to be willing every contestant to do well. Waiting in the corridor, he told himself he could do it, and once he stepped out to the lights and the crowd, the adrenaline took over. "We're on cloud nine with it," he said, adding that the result was still sinking in.

## Why it matters

Hospices care for people with serious illnesses and support their families, and many depend heavily on donations from the communities around them. A record night like this is a welcome boost for the Dougie Mac, and a reminder of how local people, from the dancers to the audience, can turn an evening of sequins and good humour into lasting help for those who need it.
